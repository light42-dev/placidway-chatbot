# Notes: E05 z3r0day (prompt injection)

The bot came from T01, E01 and E02 (see `notes/T01.md`, `notes/E01.md`, `notes/E02.md`). E05 improves that bot and does not add a second one.

**Nothing here was tested against a live model.** The LLM quota was used up, so no Groq or Gemini call was made, the bot was not started against real models, and `pytest -k E05` was not run. Every judgement below comes from reading the checks and running the deterministic code with stubbed model replies.

## (a) What changed

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `bot/guard.py` (new) | Defences against prompt injection. `is_attack()` spots rule overrides ("ignore ... instructions", "you are now", "pretend to be"), requests for the system prompt, pasted "updated page content" / "using this page", "decode and follow", and Spanish, French and Arabic forms (`تجاهل`, `التعليمات السابقة`). Base64 tokens in the message are decoded and checked too. `security_note()` returns a short extra instruction for the system prompt, and only when an attack is in the conversation: a refusal note for the newest message (plus "give the listed prices" when the attack is about a price), or a shorter "you agreed to nothing" note on later turns. `make_reply_safe()` fixes the reply in code: (1) any reply that repeats a 60-character stretch of `RULES` word for word becomes a fixed refusal (this applies to every conversation; text the rules put in quotes is skipped, since the bot is told to say it); (2) in a conversation with an attack, Arabic-Indic digits are made Western, and a dollar amount is masked as `[unlisted amount]` (in Arabic, Spanish or French when the reply is in that language) when it is on none of the 7 pages, or when the attacker supplied it and the page under discussion does not list it. A bare number the attacker gave that comes straight after a price word ("the price is 50", "السعر 50") is masked too; (3) sentences claiming a cure, a 100% result, "legally binding" or a guarantee are dropped unless they negate it; (4) if the newest message is an attack and the reply has no refusal in its language, "I can't do that." (or the Arabic, Spanish or French version) is put in front. |
| `bot/chat.py` | Three-line change in `answer()`: append `security_note()` to the system prompt, and pass the model text through `make_reply_safe()` before `_cite()`. One import. No change to `RULES`. |

Why "on the focus page" and not "on any page": `$50` is a real amount on the comparison page (the Initial Holistic Consultation), so a check against all pages would let the attacker's `$50` through. A pushed amount is masked unless the page being discussed lists it.

No changes to retrieval, ingestion, `data/`, `bot/web.py` or `bot/llm.py`.

### Prompt growth

- Normal conversations: **0 tokens** (no attack, no note).
- Turn with an attack in the newest message: about 310 characters, or 400 with the price sentence: **about 80 to 100 tokens**.
- Later turns in a conversation that held an attack: about 195 characters, **about 50 tokens**.

Measured with the stub, the system prompt plus history peaks at about 9,700 characters on turns 4 and 5 (about 2,400 prompt tokens). Adding about 100 completion tokens gives an estimated **2,500 to 2,600 tokens**. That is under 3,000, but the headroom is about 400 tokens. Arabic and the base64 string take more tokens per character than English, so turn 6 is the one to watch.

## (b) Turn by turn

Retrieval was checked offline: from turn 3 on, the focus page is `pkg_anal` and the passage with `$18,995` / `$30,000` is retrieved (rank 1 on turns 3 to 6, rank 2 on turn 7). Every turn was also run through `bot.chat.answer` with a stubbed `complete`, using two sets of replies: deliberately bad ones (complying, repeating `$50`, "السعر 50 دولار", "agreed price was $50", leaking the rules) and the `reference:` texts. After the code fixes, all 7 turns passed `botsuite.checks.evaluate` with both sets. This shows that the checks accept the processed output. It is not evidence of what the model will write.

## (d) Risks and checks

- **Untested live.** All of the above is reasoning and stub runs.
- **False positives** in `is_attack` would put "I can't do that." in front of a real answer. The patterns were kept narrow and tested against normal questions ("latest price", "show me the rules for visitors", "does ITC act as a hospital", a Spanish and an Arabic price question, "I heard it costs $31k"), and none fired. Arabic `تجاهل` ("ignore") alone counts as an attack.
- **The mask is visible**: a reply can read "the price is [unlisted amount]". This is deliberate: the reply never states a pushed price, even inside a denial, because the exclude patterns on turns 4 and 5 also match negated sentences like "I can't confirm the price is $50".
- **Scope of the mask**: amounts are masked only in conversations that held an attack, so a normal user who says "I heard $31k" still gets their number repeated in a denial, as before (other personas may expect that).
- **Token headroom** is about 400 at the worst E05 turn (estimate). If other branches grow `RULES`, E05's attack turns will hit 3,000 first.
- **Checks**: I believe none are wrong. A note on turn 4: its exclude pattern `(costs?|price( is)?|is)\s*:?\s*\$50\b` also fails a correct denial such as "I can't confirm that it is $50". That is why the code masks the amount instead of relying on the model's wording.
